Print Estimating Software: Quote Faster and Price Every Job Right

Underpricing loses margin. Overpricing loses the job. Slow quotes lose both. Here is how estimating software solves all three.

By the Pulse Print team·November 4, 2026·2 min read

Estimating is where profit is won or lost before a single sheet is printed. A quote that forgets make-ready, finishing time or spoilage quietly eats the margin. A quote that takes two days to arrive loses the job to a faster competitor. Print estimating software makes every quote fast, consistent and based on real costs.

Why manual estimating breaks down

  • It depends on one person. When the estimator is away, quotes stop.
  • It's inconsistent. Two people price the same job differently.
  • Costs change. Paper prices move, but the spreadsheet doesn't.
  • Hidden costs get missed. Make-ready, waste allowances and finishing labour are easy to forget.

What print estimating software calculates

  • Materials: stock by sheet or roll, with imposition and waste allowance
  • Ink or click charges
  • Press time: make-ready plus run time at your real machine rates
  • Finishing: cutting, folding, binding, laminating
  • Outsourced work and shipping
  • Your margin: applied consistently, with warnings when a price drops below target

The benefits

Speed

Standard jobs can be priced in seconds and complex ones in minutes. Fast replies win more work.

Accuracy

Every quote uses the same up-to-date costs and rules, so you stop underpricing complex jobs.

Quote to job in one click

Accepted quotes become job tickets automatically, carrying every spec forward. See print job ticket software.

Better decisions

Comparing estimated and actual costs shows which products are truly profitable, a core print shop KPI.

Where AI helps

AI can draft quotes from similar past jobs, flag prices that fall below your target margin, and highlight quotes worth following up. Read more in AI for print shops.

Getting started

  1. Enter your real costs: stocks, machine rates, labour and finishing.
  2. Set up your most common products as templates.
  3. Define your target margins by product or customer type.
  4. Review estimated vs. actual costs monthly and adjust.
