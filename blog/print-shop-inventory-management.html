Print Shop Inventory Management: Stop Running Out of Paper

Jobs waiting on stock, cash tied up in paper you don't use, emergency orders at premium prices. Better inventory control fixes all three.

By the Pulse Print team·November 4, 2026·2 min read

Nothing stops a job faster than discovering, on the day of the run, that the paper isn't there. On the other side, shelves full of slow-moving stock tie up cash you could use elsewhere. Good print shop inventory management keeps you between those two extremes.

What to track

  • Paper and substrates: by type, weight, size and finish
  • Inks and toners
  • Finishing supplies: laminate, binding materials, grommets
  • Large-format media: vinyl, banner, canvas
  • Packaging and shipping materials

6 steps to better inventory control

1. Know what you have

Keep a live count of every item, updated when stock arrives and when jobs consume it. If your count lives in someone's head, start here.

2. Tie stock to job tickets

When a job is scheduled, its stock should be reserved automatically. That way you see shortages days ahead, not on the morning of the run. Learn more about digital job tickets.

3. Set reorder points

For each item, set a minimum level based on how fast you use it and how long your supplier takes to deliver. When stock drops below it, reorder.

4. Automate reorders

Reorder alerts (or automatically drafted purchase orders) remove the risk of someone forgetting.

5. Forecast demand

Seasonal peaks and regular customer orders are predictable. Using order history (and AI forecasting) helps you buy ahead of busy periods.

6. Manage vendors in one place

Compare prices and lead times, send requests for quotes, and track orders from each supplier without digging through email.

Inventory and waste

Better inventory control also reduces waste: fewer emergency substitutions, less expired stock and better use of offcuts. See our guide to reducing print production waste for more.

Signs you need a system

  • Jobs are delayed because stock ran out
  • You place rush orders at premium prices
  • Nobody knows exactly what is on the shelves
  • Stock counts happen in spreadsheets, if at all
