How to Reduce Production Waste in Your Print Shop

Spoilage, make-ready, overruns and reprints quietly eat your margin. Here is how to measure them, and bring them down.

By the Pulse Print team·November 4, 2026·2 min read

Waste is one of the biggest hidden costs in printing. A few spoiled sheets per run doesn't look like much, but across hundreds of jobs a month it adds up to real money in paper, ink and press time. The good news: most print production waste comes from a handful of causes you can measure and fix.

Know where your waste comes from

Common sources include:

  • Make-ready: sheets used to get colour and registration right.
  • Spoilage during the run: misfeeds, marks, colour drift.
  • Overruns: printing more than needed "just in case".
  • Reprints: wrong file, missed proof change, finishing errors.
  • Poor sheet usage: small jobs printed alone instead of ganged.

9 steps to reduce production waste

1. Measure spoilage per job

You can't fix what you don't measure. Record planned vs. actual sheets on every job, by press and operator. Within a few weeks, patterns appear.

2. Lock down proof approval

Most reprints start with a proof: an unlogged change, an old file version, an approval given by phone. Online proofing with version control and a recorded sign-off removes the guesswork.

3. Send the right file to press, every time

A single source for approved artwork means operators never pick "FINAL_v2" when "FINAL_v3" was approved.

4. Schedule to reduce changeovers

Grouping jobs by stock, size and ink coverage cuts make-ready sheets and time. This is exactly the kind of decision AI scheduling handles well.

5. Gang compatible jobs

Combining small jobs that share paper and finishing on one sheet uses less stock and fewer press runs.

6. Set realistic overs

Base overs on actual spoilage history per product and press instead of a blanket percentage.

7. Keep presses calibrated

Rising spoilage on one machine is often an early sign it needs calibration or maintenance. Track it so you act before it gets expensive.

8. Standardise substrates and settings

Fewer stock variations and saved press settings mean faster, more repeatable make-ready.

9. Review waste weekly

Make waste a standing item in your weekly meeting: top three jobs, machines and causes. Small, steady fixes compound.

Turn waste data into decisions

The shops that cut waste fastest are the ones that can see it. When every job's planned and actual sheets, reprints and press times live in print shop automation software, waste becomes a number on a dashboard instead of a feeling.
